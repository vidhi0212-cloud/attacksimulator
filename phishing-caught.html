What Gave This Email Away
THE EMAIL YOU RECEIVED HAD THESE RED FLAGS:
FROM:security-alert@noreply-verification.net
SUBJECT:⚠️ Urgent: Account verification required
LINK:http://verify-account.suspicious-domain.com/...
TONE:Urgency + threat of account suspension
1
Suspicious sender domain
The email came from a domain you don't recognise. Your company's IT team would send from an official @yourcompany.com address — never a generic or hyphenated domain.
2
Artificial urgency
Phrases like "verify within 24 hours" or "account will be suspended" are designed to make you act without thinking. Legitimate services don't threaten sudden suspension without prior notice.
3
Generic greeting
Phishing emails often use generic greetings or may have slightly off formatting. Your organisation's systems know your name and role.
4
Link doesn't match the claimed sender
Before clicking any link, hover over it to preview the URL. If the domain doesn't match the claimed sender's organisation, it's almost certainly a phishing attempt.
5
Requests credential input
Legitimate services rarely ask you to "verify your account" by clicking a link and entering your password. Go directly to the service's official website instead.
What You Should Do Next Time
✦
DON'T CLICK — When in doubt, do not click links in emails. Go directly to the website by typing the URL in your browser.
✦
HOVER FIRST — Hover over any link before clicking to preview the real destination URL in your browser's status bar.
✦
REPORT IT — Forward suspicious emails to security@yourcompany.com before deleting. Your report protects your colleagues too.
✦
VERIFY OUT-OF-BAND — If an email requests urgent action, call the sender directly using a known phone number — not a number given in the email.
✦
ENABLE MFA — Multi-factor authentication significantly limits the damage even if you do click a phishing link and enter credentials.
